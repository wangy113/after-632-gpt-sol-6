632 CE — Succession

Muhammad died in 632. Leading members of the community chose his companion Abu Bakr as caliph, or leader. For them, the community could select its leader. Supporters of Ali, Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law, believed leadership belonged with the Prophet's family, and some held that Muhammad had named Ali. Each view answered: what makes authority rightful?

661 CE — Political struggle

Ali became caliph in 656, but his rule was contested and civil war followed. In 661 a Khariji, from a group that had broken with Ali, killed him. Mu'awiya, Syria's governor, became caliph and ruled from Damascus. His Umayyad family kept power and ended selection by consensus. The disagreement over leadership had become a contest for political power.

680 CE — Karbala and remembrance

In 680 Mu'awiya's son Yazid became caliph. Husayn, Ali's son and Muhammad's grandson, refused to accept him. At Karbala, in Iraq, Yazid's forces killed Husayn and a small group of his family and followers. Shi'a Muslims came to remember his death as a turning point: a stand for rightful leadership against injustice. They commemorate it each year on Ashura.
