01 BEFORE WE BEGIN

Different traditions.
Shared foundations.

Sunni and Shi'a Muslims belong to Islam and share central beliefs and practices, including belief in one God, the Qur'an, and prayer. Their traditions developed over time.

These three dates illuminate origins. They do not explain every later difference—or a conflict in today's news.

MOMENT 01 / OF 03
632CE

A question of succession

After Muhammad died in 632, Abu Bakr became the first caliph, the leader of the Muslim community. Supporters of Ali understood rightful leadership differently. The question of who should lead, and on what grounds, would shape later disagreements within Islam.

A little more context +

These dates illuminate origins, not a fully formed pair of denominations. Sunni and Shi'a traditions developed over centuries, including differences in religious authority and practice. This short experience leaves much of that later history for further reading.

Source: Parkinson, chapter 8
MOMENT 02 / OF 03
661CE

Authority in dispute

Ali died in 661, and Umayyad rule took shape under Mu'awiya. Disagreement over leadership developed through political struggles within the Muslim community. These events were part of a longer history; Sunni and Shi'a traditions did not emerge fully formed in a single moment.

A little more context +

The courtyard stays the same because this is a history within a shared Muslim community. The changing light is a metaphor for time, not a reconstruction of an event. The textbook's sections 8.8 and 8.9 provide a fuller account of the political struggles.

Source: Parkinson, chapter 8
MOMENT 03 / OF 03
680CE

A memory that endures

Husayn, Muhammad's grandson and Ali's son, was killed at Karbala in 680. His death became central to Shi'a remembrance of injustice and rightful leadership. Here, changes in light mark a moment for reflection; this imagined courtyard does not recreate Karbala or its violence.

A little more context +

Karbala became central to Shi'a memory and commemoration. These three dates are a starting point, not a complete account of Sunni and Shi'a belief or practice. The source below offers further context, including later developments in the history of Islam.
