# Current prototype: three-date image review

**Status: prepared; no participant sessions run.** Use this packet with 3 to 5 students or colleagues to learn what they notice and infer from the current textured room. It complements the [gray-box packet](GRAYBOX_REVIEW_PACKET.md) and the separate [680 floor-mark variant sheet](680_FLOOR_MARK_REVIEW_SHEET.md). The room and its objects are imagined visual interpretations, not a reconstruction or evidence of historical belongings.

## Prepare

- Allow about 20–30 minutes per person for the image-only pass, plus 5–10 minutes if using the optional reading pass. Give each person an anonymous code and a copy of the blank response sheet below.
- Show the images at the same size on one display. Within each viewpoint, show 632, then 661, then 680. Show one image at a time for its first response; only then place it beside the previous date. Use the four viewpoints in this order: doorway, travel corner, pegs wall, yard.
- Keep this packet, filenames, scene notes, passages, and the intended visual cues out of view until the image-only answers are recorded. Do not point to an object or name an intended symbol during the first pass. Record the participant's exact words and uncertainty without correcting them.
- All twelve images are matched, normal **shadow-free** views. The 680 doorway and travel images use the current `0.45` floor mark. Keep the opt-in `0.30` treatments for the separate floor-mark study.

Say: “These show the same **imagined** place at three dates, from several viewpoints. It is not a reconstruction of a documented room. Please tell me what you notice and what you think the changes might suggest. ‘I can't tell’ is useful too.”

## Image-only pass

For the first image in each viewpoint, ask: “What do you notice here? What, if anything, does it suggest to you? What in the image gives you that idea? What is unclear?” For 661 and 680, also ask: “What changed from the previous image? What, if anything, might that change suggest?” Record the response before putting the two dates side by side. Repeat the same open questions for all four viewpoints, without naming a prop or offering an explanation.

After all twelve first responses, let the participant compare the three dates within any viewpoint. Ask: “Which differences matter most to you? What seems to stay the same? Did another viewpoint change your reading? Is there anything you would need to know before drawing a conclusion?” If more detail is needed, ask only: “Can you point to what gave you that idea?”

## Optional reading-context pass

Only after the image-only pass, show the [dated passages and “In this room” / “In the record” notes](https://wangy113.github.io/after-632-gpt-sol-6/#reading). Ask: “Does the text change how you understand an image? Which details now seem helpful or confusing? Can you tell which scene details are imagined and which statements draw on sources?” Record revised answers separately. This pass checks clarity of the distinction; it does not turn an image-only inference into an unprompted one.

## Facilitator coding · after the participant leaves

Use the [Phase 0 plan](../PLAN.md#the-experience) only now to compare intended cues with what was actually said. Separately note whether someone noticed the 632 loose mats and empty head position, the 661 facing arrangement and travel things, and the 680 missing travel things, floor trace, empty hook, or taller palm. Mark the viewpoint and whether each observation was spontaneous, prompted, or absent. The small letter and coin are not required identifications from these images. Do not score a historical event or person as “recognized” from an uncaptioned room.

The yard frame is a close turned view and crops the palm crown at its upper edge in each date. Record whether anyone notices a change; do not treat the image difference alone as proof that they understood the intended taller-tree cue.

After 3 to 5 sessions, summarize counts, exact examples of contrasting interpretations, confusion caused by the texture or viewpoint, and one revision to consider. Leave findings blank until people have taken part. This image packet does not establish historical suitability, screen-reader behavior, or real-device performance.
