# 680 floor-mark review sheet

**Status: prepared; no sessions run.** Use this short visual check with 3 to 5 students or colleagues. It asks how people read three treatments of the same imagined 680 room. It does not replace the [three-date gray-box review](GRAYBOX_REVIEW_PACKET.md), which asks what changes from 661 to 680. Record participants' words before deciding whether to change the mark.

## Facilitator setup

- Show the six [matched shadow-free captures](GEOMETRY_BASELINE.md#680-shadow-free-floor-mark-review--2026-10-15) on the same display at the same size. Each card has a doorway view and a view from the head-of-room travel corner. These are **alternative renderings of one date**, not successive dates.
- Keep this sheet, image filenames, URLs, option names, scene notes, and the intended reading out of the participant's view. Show one image at a time for first impressions. Do not point to the floor or explain the scene before recording those impressions.
- Give each participant an anonymous code. Rotate card order: participants 1–3 see A–B–C, B–C–A, and C–A–B respectively; participants 4–5, if recruited, see A–C–B and C–B–A. Within each card, show doorway first, then travel corner.

Say: “These are views of the same **imagined** room at one date, shown in a few visual versions. It is not a reconstruction of a documented place. Tell me what you notice and what it might suggest. ‘I can't tell’ is useful too.”

## First impressions: one card at a time

For each card, show its doorway image alone. Ask: “What do you notice? What, if anything, does this view suggest to you? What in the image gives you that idea? What is unclear?” Record the answer before showing the travel-corner image. Then ask: “Does this second view change or add to your reading? What makes you say that?” Repeat for the remaining cards in the assigned order. Do not name the floor mark or its intended meaning during this pass.

## Comparison, then optional floor prompt

After all six first impressions, show the three doorway images together at the same size, then the three travel-corner images together. Ask: “What, if anything, differs among these versions? Does any difference change how you read the room? Which details help or get in the way?” Record the participant's words and any uncertainty. Do not present a version as preferred.

Only **after** the open questions, if the participant has not discussed the relevant floor area, point to it and ask: “What do you make of this area of floor?” Mark that answer as **prompted**, separate from what they noticed without a hint. Do not suggest “cleaned,” “missing,” or another interpretation in the question.
